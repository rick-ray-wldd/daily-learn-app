You already told us what you didn’t understand.

Language learners hit rewind dozens of times a day, and no app records that tap. Echo keeps the fifteen seconds you replayed — no questions, no interruption — and hands them back tomorrow.

Playing. The learner does nothing extra.
Window [T−15, T] — the fifteen seconds we keep.
+6 s of context each side — 27 s to replay.
01 / AS IT HAPPENS
Catch the tap
Back button, Control Center, lock screen. Taps within 3 s count once.
02 / SECONDS LATER
Transcribe just this
Windowed ASR — not the whole episode. Cheaper, and no waiting.
03 / THAT EVENING
Diagnose the break
Vocabulary, linking, speed, grammar, accent, culture.
04 / NEXT MORNING
It comes to you
A notification carries the word to the lock screen. Three choices, plus “can’t recall”.
05 / AFTER THAT
Before you forget
Spaced repetition; every grade leaves a review log.

Why the rewind button is a good signal

3.06ODDS RATIO
Learners who spontaneously noticed a vocabulary hole were 3.06× as likely to learn that word.
4.57ODDS RATIO
Learners prompted to speak, and who noticed the gap that way: 4.57×.
n.s.BETWEEN THE TWO
The two groups did not differ significantly — forcing the action beats noticing on your own by nothing.
What does the work is realising you have a gap — not the action.
Hitting rewind is that moment. So we never interrupt.
34–38%VARIANCE EXPLAINED
Aural vocabulary + lexical segmentation explain this much of L2 listening.
0RIVALS COLLECTING IT
No app collects where the words stop being separable. Text has no connected speech.

Noticing vocabulary holes aids incidental L2 word learning — Bilingualism: Lang. & Cognition · Field (2003), Lexical Segmentation in L2 Listening

Not an English product. A listening one.

The market is language learning. English is just my own gap — a rewind carries no language. Swap the speech model and the same loop runs on Mandarin, Japanese or Spanish.
